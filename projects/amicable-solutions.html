Designing Self-Serve Dispute Resolution for C2C Transactions

The problem

When a Kleinanzeigen transaction went wrong — item not as described, late delivery, no response — both buyers and sellers had one option: contact support. The same handful of dispute scenarios were generating thousands of support contacts every month, and each case required a human agent to triage and chase both parties.

The cost was real. So was the user experience: waiting days for a first response on a problem that, in most cases, had a clear resolution path.

My role

Senior Product Designer. I led end-to-end design from discovery through handoff — dispute taxonomy, resolution flow, dispute center UI, and the buyer–seller communication layer. I worked across product, legal, and trust & safety engineering.

What made this hard

This wasn't a feature with a clear brief. The starting point was a problem space — too many support contacts, no self-serve path — and the design work had to define the solution space before it could design anything.

Legal and trust & safety had legitimate constraints that sometimes pulled against the simplest UX path. Finding the resolution framework that satisfied compliance requirements without causing users to abandon the flow was the central design challenge.

Decisions I drove

The classification framework — defining which dispute types could be resolved through self-serve and which required escalation — was not a product decision handed to me. I led that work: combing through support ticket data, then mapping each scenario to a resolution path with the minimum viable number of steps.

The core design principle I pushed for: the flow should guide both parties toward the fastest fair outcome, not just give the platform legal cover.

Approach

Most recurring cases fell into a small number of categories: item significantly not as described, item not received, partial refund disagreements, and no-contact situations. From those I mapped resolution paths and minimum steps. The resulting design included a dispute initiation flow, structured in-app communication, a dispute center for tracking, and a clear escalation path.

Impact

40% of disputes resolved through self-serve in early rollout, without human agent involvement
€400K projected annual savings in support costs, modelled from month-one data
↓ significantly reduced average first-response time — from days to immediate guided action
