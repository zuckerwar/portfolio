Motion

Motion as a Design Discipline, Not a Finishing Touch

Before I was a product designer, I was an animator. I spent several years working in broadcast — character animation, title sequences, motion graphics — and that background has never really left how I work.

Most product designers treat motion as something you add at the end: a transition here, a loading state there, maybe a micro-interaction if time allows. I treat it as a design material that belongs in the process from the start. The timing of a state change tells the user what kind of change it was. The easing curve on a panel expansion tells them whether it's appearing from somewhere or appearing from nothing. These things communicate.

At XING, I defined the motion foundations layer as part of the design system work — easing curves, duration guidelines, usage principles — so that animation decisions were consistent and intentional across the product rather than left to individual engineers or designers to improvise.

At Google, I designed and produced the Snapseed launch animation in After Effects, outputting platform-specific versions for iOS and Android.

The pieces here are personal work — experiments made outside of product projects, where the only brief is what's interesting. They're a record of how I think about timing, character, and the specific kind of life you can give to something that isn't a static image.
