Product Design · Google · 2016

Redesigning Google Snapseed for a Broader Audience Without Losing Its Depth

Context

Snapseed was already a critically acclaimed photo editing app — later cited as a major influence on Google Photos. The challenge for this redesign wasn't to reinvent it, but to extend its reach: make it genuinely approachable for casual users discovering it for the first time, while keeping the gesture-based depth that power users loved.

My role

User Experience Designer. I was responsible for the information architecture of the editing experience, the tool and filter discovery redesign, the icon system, the launch screen animation, and the visual alignment to Google's Material Design language.

Design goals

  • Lower the entry barrier — reduce the learning curve without tutorial screens or tooltips
  • Improve discoverability of tools and filters without removing gesture-based controls
  • Redesign the parameter selector interaction to be more immediately legible
  • Align visual language with Google Material Design across iOS and Android
  • Introduce a new "Double Exposure" filter within the updated system

Launch screen

Replacing the static logo on the launch screen with an animated sequence designed in After Effects. The animation served a functional purpose — it gave the app a moment to initialize while giving new users a first impression of the product's craft and personality. Two separate outputs were produced, one optimized for iOS rendering and one for Android.

Discovery redesign

The original filter and tool interface presented everything at once, which overwhelmed new users. The redesign organized tools into a clearer categorical structure with progressive disclosure — new users see the most-used tools first, with full depth available one level down.

Icon system

Rebuilt the full icon set from scratch to ensure optical consistency — consistent stroke weight, sizing on the pixel grid, and visual weight across the full library. Icons that had been drawn at different scales and weights over time were unified.
