Designing a Data-Heavy Internal Platform for Banking Advisors

Context

Banking advisors at Deutsche Bank were operating with a patchwork of legacy tools — each internally consistent, none communicating with each other. Client data lived in one place, portfolio information in another, transaction history somewhere else. Every client meeting started with the same preparation ritual: pulling information from multiple systems and assembling it manually.

This project, run via HCL Technologies, was to design a unified internal platform that brought the full advisor workflow into a single, coherent digital environment.

The design challenge

Internal tooling for financial professionals sits at a specific intersection of constraints: the data is inherently dense, the stakes of misreading it are high, and the users are experts who have no patience for interfaces that slow them down. The design challenge is how to present high-density data in a way that's fast to scan, accurate to interpret, and aligned to the workflow it supports.

Alongside that, the platform had to carry Deutsche Bank's brand credibility. Advisors present this interface to clients. It had to feel modern and trustworthy, not like internal tooling.

My approach

I spent significant time in the discovery phase shadowing advisors and walking through their actual daily workflows before touching visual design. Understanding the sequence — how they prepare for a client meeting, what they need during it, what they do immediately after — shaped the information hierarchy more than any best-practice heuristic would have.

For the data tables and charts: the design principle was accuracy first, then speed of scanning. Color was used semantically (not decoratively) to mark status and directionality. Typography hierarchy within tables was kept tight — the contrast between label and value mattered more than visual breathing room.

Brand alignment to Deutsche Bank's visual identity was handled at the token level — color, typography, and spacing — so the platform felt of a piece with other Deutsche Bank touchpoints without attempting a full consumer-grade visual redesign.
